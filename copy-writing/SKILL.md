# Copy writing

Sixteen rules. Every line of customer-facing copy passes all sixteen before it ships.
This governs the words, not the code, and not chat replies to Ishan (caveman governs those).

## The sixteen

**1. Sell the end state.** Write the reader's life after the product works, not the product.
"Your dog walks 3km before you finish coffee" beats "GPS walk tracking with route history".
The feature is the receipt, the end state is the sale.

**2. Pass the stranger test.** Give the line to somebody who has never heard of the product.
If they cannot say what it does and who it is for, the line failed. No inside vocabulary,
no acronym the reader has to already know, no reference to a screen they have not seen.

**3. Cut, then cut again.** Write it, halve it, halve what survives. Every word left must
be load-bearing. If deleting a word does not change the meaning, the word was decoration.

**4. Select your reader.** Good copy repels the wrong reader on purpose. Name the person,
the situation or the constraint so the right reader feels seen and everybody else moves on.
Copy written for everyone converts no one.

**5. No em dashes, no en dashes.** Anywhere. Commas, periods, parentheses, colons, hyphens.
This is the single loudest tell of machine text.

**6. Tone matches slot.** A push notification, a hero headline, an App Store subtitle and a
cold email are four different registers with four different lengths. Write for the slot the
line lives in, including its character limit, not for a generic "brand voice".

**7. Kill empty hedges.** Delete "helps you", "designed to", "can assist with", "aims to",
"seamlessly", "effortlessly", "simply". They exist to avoid a claim. Make the claim or cut
the line.

**8. Angles, not synonyms.** When asked for variants, change the argument, not the wording.
Five lines that say the same thing in different adjectives are one line. Vary the angle:
fear, status, time saved, money, identity, the objection, the specific moment of use.

**9. One idea each.** One line carries one idea. Two ideas in a headline means neither lands.
Split them, or drop the weaker one.

**10. Never invent claims.** No number, percentage, award, user count, testimonial, integration
or capability that was not given. Ask for the fact or write around it. A fabricated claim is a
lie the client ships in public.

**11. Truth beats style.** When a clever line needs a small distortion to work, kill the line.
Accuracy is the constraint, wit is the optimization inside it.

**12. Benefit, not mechanism.** The reader buys the outcome, not the architecture. "Notes are
there on your phone before you sit down" beats "real-time sync over WebSocket". Mechanism goes
in the body only when it is the proof for a benefit already stated.

**13. Numbers beat adjectives.** "Fast" is an opinion, "opens in 200ms" is a fact. Replace
"powerful", "robust", "advanced" and "significant" with the real number whenever there is one.
No real number and no way to get one means cut the adjective, not invent the number.

**14. Front-load the point.** The first three words carry the message. No throat-clearing
opener, no scene-setting clause, no "In today's world". Readers scan the start of a line and
leave.

**15. Sound unmistakably brand.** The line should be impossible to paste into a competitor's
page unchanged. Swap the logo test: if the copy still works for a rival, it says nothing
specific and the work is not done.

**16. No AI lexicon.** Banned outright: delve, elevate, unlock, unleash, seamless, robust,
leverage, harness, empower, transform, revolutionize, game-changer, cutting-edge, in today's
fast-paced world, it's not just X, it's Y, the result? , imagine a world where, dive in,
supercharge, tailored, curated, holistic, journey, landscape, realm, testament, navigate the
complexities. Also banned: the rule-of-three list where the third item adds nothing, and the
one-word sentence used as a drum beat. Period.

## Structural tells to avoid

- Every sentence the same length. Vary it, real writing has rhythm.
- A colon in the middle of every headline.
- Perfect parallel structure across all bullets in a list.
- A closing line that summarizes what the reader just read.
- Rhetorical questions used as a transition.
- The bolded first phrase of every bullet, in body copy that is not a spec sheet.

## Working method

1. Get the facts first: who the reader is, what changes for them, what is provably true.
   Missing facts get asked for, never invented (rule 10).
2. Write more variants than asked for, each a different angle (rule 8).
3. Run the checklist below on each survivor.
4. Deliver the shortlist plus one line each on the angle it plays. Never deliver a single
   option and never deliver twenty.

## Checklist before delivery

- [ ] End state visible, not just the feature
- [ ] A stranger understands it cold
- [ ] Cut twice, every word load-bearing
- [ ] A specific reader is selected
- [ ] Zero em dashes or en dashes
- [ ] Length and register fit the actual slot
- [ ] No hedge verbs
- [ ] Variants differ by angle, not vocabulary
- [ ] One idea per line
- [ ] Every claim traceable to a supplied fact
- [ ] No accuracy traded for wit
- [ ] Benefit leads, mechanism supports
- [ ] Adjectives replaced with numbers where numbers exist
- [ ] First three words carry the point
- [ ] Fails the competitor swap test
- [ ] Zero words from the banned list
